# What Did Ancient Humans Do When It Rained All Week? (v2: the score)

Supersedes the line order in `rain_week_draft.md` (sources and fact checks there still apply). This is the **idea → voiceover with timestamps** step. Nothing is voiced or built until this is approved.

## The idea

> When it rains for a week, a camp has three jobs: **stay warm, stay fed, stay together.** The first two keep you alive. The third is what you still do.

Every section of the video answers one of the three, and the close calls them back. If a line does not serve one of them, it is cut.

| Job | Days | What the viewer learns |
|---|---|---|
| Warm | 1 shelter, 2 fire | wet clothes steal heat; huts of branch or mammoth bone; never let the fire die |
| Fed | 3 stored food, 4 mending | smoked and dried meat; honey and tubers (Hadza); the last strip gone on day six; work that fixes what the rain wrecked |
| Together | night, 6, 7 | firelight talk (Wiessner); lamps and paintings; the river rises and the camp climbs the hill together |

## How this is timed

- **One phrase per voice line.** Every sentence, and sometimes half a sentence, is its own Azure clip, so every silence is a real, measured gap in the timeline and not a pause the voice invented.
- **Silence is written down.** The `then` column is the quiet after the line, in seconds, with the rain still going. Reveals get the longest ones.
- **One idea per shot,** 3–6 s. A picture lands about 0.3 s *before* its word, never on it or after.
- Times below are estimates (about 0.4 s a word). The real ones come from Azure's word boundaries when we voice it, and the shots are cut to those.

## Part 2 · the three jobs (outline; scored line by line once Part 1 is approved)

**Warm**
- Day 1 · "Day one. First job: get out of the wet." Wet clothes steal body heat [I]. Then "A cave, if you were lucky." (shelter 1) / "If not, a hut." (shelter 2) with Mezhyrich as a map pin [E; hides "probably"].
- Day 2 · "Day two. The fire." Sparks from pyrite and flint [E]. "Sparks and wet wood don't mix. So: never let it go out." [I] A person feeding it; the dry-wood stack shrinks by a log a day.

**Fed**
- Day 3 · smoked and dried meat [E/debated, said as such]; the Hadza in the wet season lean on honey, tubers and fruit [C; map pin Tanzania].
- Day 4 · "A wet day is a work day." Scraping skins, sharpening, mending; eyed needles by about 26,000 years ago [E].
- Day 6 · the last strip of meat comes off the rack. The count on the rack and the tally on the stone agree.

**Together**
- Night · "Then the sun goes down, and the fire changes the conversation." Wiessner: by day, gossip, complaints and trade; by night, 81% stories [C; to verify against the paper]. Speech bubbles: small by day, large and painted by night.
- Lamps · Lascaux lamps [E; count to verify]; "We can't say the paintings were made in the rain. But you needed time, and light." [I]
- Day 7 · the river rises, the camp climbs the hill together; Ohalo II as the camp the water kept [E].
- Close · the clouds break. "Warm. Fed. Together." A week indoors, the fire, the stories, each other. "Which sounds a lot like ours."

## Maps

Real places are shown on maps, not named in a caption: Europe (the opening), Ukraine (Mezhyrich), Tanzania (Hadza), Israel (Ohalo II), France (Lascaux), Botswana/Namibia (Ju/'hoansi). Outlines come from **Natural Earth** (public domain), simplified to fit the style and baked into Dart paths so rendering needs no data files. Natural Earth has modern coastlines only; at 20,000 years ago sea level was about 120 m lower and the North Sea was land. The Europe map is drawn with today's coastline and a small "today's coastlines" note rather than a guess at the old one. The ice sheet is a hand-placed approximation of its extent and is labelled as one.

## The two looks

Same script, same timings, same characters and walks. Only the drawing differs:

- **Explainer look** (what exists): soft gradients, layered depth, light and weather as atmosphere.
- **Doodle look**: thick dark outlines, flat colour, round heads, simple limbs, hand-lettered type, paper-cut maps.

The opening is rendered in both and you pick one; Part 2 follows in the winner.
